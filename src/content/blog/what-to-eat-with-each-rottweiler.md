Good pairing is not about rules. It is about two things that make each other better. Here is where we start with each beer.

## Strong Lager, The Guardian

A full, smooth 7.5% lager wants food with weight. Think tandoori chicken straight off the coals, seekh kebabs or a slow mutton biryani. The bready malt sits under the char. The soft bitterness keeps your mouth clean between bites.

## Dry Lager, The Runner

This is the one for heat. Chilli and bitterness push each other up, so a low-bitterness, very dry lager is the calmest friend a spicy plate can have. Try it with a Chettinad chicken, a Goan prawn curry or a plate of vada pav. The crisp bubbles do the cooling.

## IPA, The Bite

An IPA is bold, so give it something rich that can stand its ground. Butter chicken, malai kofta and paneer tikka all work. The citrus and resin cut through the cream. One honest warning: bitterness makes chilli feel hotter, so keep the IPA for the mild, rich dishes and save the fiery ones for the Dry Lager.

## Witbier, The Sunbather

Coriander and orange peel are already in the glass, so lean into them. Fish with a green chutney, a Kerala moilee, chaat or a light salad on a hot afternoon. A soft, hazy witbier also works well with lunch, when you want something easy.

## A simple way to remember it

Match strength with strength. Cool heat with the crispest beer on the table. Pair the hoppy one with richness. Let the light one share the table with light food.
